How I Work
"Most organizations know where they want to go. What's usually missing is the architecture: clarity, accountability, and a shared understanding of who does what, how, and why."

My work sits in the gap between strategy and behavior. Leadership sets direction; I build the operating infrastructure that makes it real: governance structures, team charters, escalation frameworks, operating rhythms, and the communication systems that carry direction consistently through an organization. When those systems exist, alignment stops depending on heroics and starts being how the organization runs.

I lead through influence rather than hierarchy. Embedded alongside GMs, VPs, and functional leaders, I facilitate the strategy sessions, coach the managers, design the frameworks, and hold the follow-through, so the work continues and scales beyond any single initiative or person.

AI-Enhanced Practice

Where AI enters the work

AI doesn't generate organizational wisdom. It multiplies the capacity of someone who already has it. Four domains where I leverage AI tools daily.

01
Executive Communication & C-Suite Ghostwriting
How I use AI
Draft-to-refined in a single working session. I use AI to generate first-pass board materials, all-hands narratives, and leadership communications, then apply the executive's voice, organizational context, and political awareness that only a trusted insider can provide.
What it produces
Board decks, CEO all-hands scripts, VP communications, and change narratives that used to require multiple revision cycles now move from brief to final-quality draft in hours, without losing the human voice or organizational intelligence at the center.
02
Organizational Diagnostics & Pattern Synthesis
How I use AI
Engagement survey themes, leadership interview notes, and meeting transcripts that used to require days of synthesis get analyzed for patterns, gaps, and root causes in a structured working session. I frame the diagnostic brief, interrogate the output, and apply contextual interpretation AI cannot access.
What it produces
Diagnostic summaries with pattern analysis, prioritized organizational themes, and recommended intervention frameworks, delivered faster and with more structural precision than manual synthesis alone.
03
Governance & Operating Framework Design
How I use AI
When designing team charters, accountability structures, operating rhythms, or decision frameworks, I use AI to generate structural options, then pressure-test them against the specific organizational context, culture, and leadership dynamics I know firsthand.
What it produces
HPT charters, governance frameworks, escalation matrices, and operating cadence designs that are structurally sound and contextually calibrated, not generic templates.
04
Change Management Planning
How I use AI
Stakeholder analysis, change narrative development, communication sequencing, and resistance mapping: AI generates comprehensive structural scaffolding quickly. I apply the organizational intelligence: who needs to hear what, when, from whom, and in what sequence.
What it produces
Change management plans, stakeholder communication matrices, and adoption roadmaps that move from concept to deployable framework in days, not weeks.

In Practice

A prompt in action

What sophisticated AI fluency looks like: not "write me a framework" but precision prompting that produces deployable work.

Sample Prompt · Organizational Diagnostic Brief
Active Use
You are helping me prepare a diagnostic summary for a 400-person global Quality & Compliance division following an engagement survey cycle. The division operates across 12 countries with a matrix structure and a historically compliance-heavy culture attempting to shift toward higher engagement and shared accountability. Survey themes indicate: strong individual role clarity, weak cross-functional collaboration, low confidence in manager communication, and variable understanding of how team contributions connect to divisional strategy. Draft a diagnostic summary with three sections: (1) key findings with root cause framing, (2) recommended priority areas for intervention, ranked by organizational impact rather than ease of implementation, and (3) a change narrative paragraph leadership can use in a follow-up all-hands. Write for a VP audience. Be specific. Avoid generic recommendations.
Why this works
The prompt provides organizational context AI cannot infer, specifies the audience and output format, defines the ranking criteria explicitly, and instructs away from the generic output most prompts produce. Precision in = precision out.
Boundaries of the Practice

What AI can and cannot do in this work

What AI does well

  • Generate structural scaffolding for frameworks and documents rapidly
  • Surface patterns across large bodies of text or data
  • Draft communication at volume without losing coherence
  • Pressure-test a framework by generating counterarguments
  • Produce multiple structural alternatives for comparison
  • Accelerate first-draft quality on complex documents

What only the practitioner can do

  • Read the real dynamics of a leadership team
  • Know which conversation needs to happen before the deck lands
  • Hold the room in a facilitated offsite when tension surfaces
  • Calibrate a message to the specific culture and political moment
  • Earn the trust that makes executive partnership possible
  • Decide what the organization needs vs. what it's asking for
