About

Give me a problem, and I’ll find a solution.

I’ve spent more than fifteen years working alongside executives in aerospace, life sciences, legal, nonprofit, and financial services. Some problems show up in every industry, like headcount and business development. Others are unique to the work. Either way, I’m at my most engaged when a leadership discussion escalates and stops making progress. I’m an optimist that way: a stalled problem is an invitation.

My approach starts with listening. In one organization, breakdowns in engagement, quality, and understanding were pushing the business in the wrong direction, and leadership couldn’t see why. I researched what was happening and held informal focus groups with the underperforming team. Then I showed leaders what I saw and heard, and designed a plan they could implement systematically to turn it around.

That’s the pattern in all of my work: understand what is really happening, make it visible to the people who can act on it, and build the systems, communications, and follow-through that make change stick.

Leading Change to a New Strategic Direction

A 90-day, ADKAR-aligned campaign to move a global workforce from awareness to adoption of a new strategic direction. It answers three questions for every employee: where are we headed, why does it matter, and what is my role.

  • Lead with decisions: show real choices and tradeoffs, not slogans.
  • Equip managers first: a briefing, toolkit, and 30-minute team conversation guide.
  • Listen and report back: pulses and listening sessions with a visible “you said, we did” loop.
  • Measure what matters: understanding, adoption, and trust, not only reach.

AI Practice

AI doesn’t supply judgment. It multiplies the capacity of someone who already has it.

I use AI every day to draft, analyze, and build faster, and I bring the context about people, culture, and timing that the tools cannot.

AI Essentials for BusinessHarvard Business School Online · Professors Karim Lakhani and Marco Iansiti · Completed 2026

I enrolled expecting a course about technology and found a course about organizations. Three takeaways shape how I work:

  1. The hardest part of becoming an AI-powered organization is redesigning the operating model so data, decisions, and learning can flow, not the algorithms.
  2. AI raises the stakes on leadership. Governance, ethics, and change management decide whether the technology creates value or chaos.
  3. The organizations that win will be the ones whose culture lets people put AI to work.
